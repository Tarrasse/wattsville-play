Where power began

Survey grid, 10° squares · the cable joins the cities you played, in date order

How to play

  • Each city makes its power one way: water, coal, wind, nuclear or gas. Each kind of plant has a rule for where it can stand. The bar at the bottom has one plant for each color. Drag a plant onto the map to build it: the square shows green where it can stand, red where it cannot. Or tap the plant (or press 1 to 4), then tap a lit square. A locked plant shows the week its customers arrive.
  • You run the power company. Every lit home pays you $0.25 a second; light every home for ×1.25.
  • Drag from a plant, home or cable to lay cable ($1 a square, $8 more over water). To remove cable, switch the toggle above the plants to Erase (or press E, or drag with the right mouse button) and drag over the cable. Erasing refunds it. After you build a plant, the toggle goes back to Cable.
  • Each plant powers as many homes as its pips. Plants cost money: when a color shows short (in the HUD and on its plant in the bar, with the number of missing pips), build or upgrade one. Too many homes on one network causes a brownout: they all dim and pay less. Route homes to plants with free pips, join same-color plants, or buy upgrades and new plants.
  • Each color is a voltage for one kind of customer: amber 220 V for homes, blue 380 V for factories, magenta 600 V DC for tram depots, green DC for charging stations. Factories and depots use 2 pips but pay more.
  • Where two cables of different colors cross, the game adds a crossover for you ($10). Erase the cable there and you get it back.
  • Hold a plant for a moment to upgrade it: +2 pips of capacity. Tap a plant to see what it costs and earns, and to upgrade or sell it (you get half of its price back).
  • Stars count your profit: the most cash you have in the run, less the money you started with. Spare pips and long cables cost money every week, so a lean network earns more.
  • From week 2, customers buy new appliances and need more power (the dots above them). Every week ends with bills for your cable, bridges, crossovers and plants. Coal, gas and nuclear plants also pay for the fuel they burn: the more power they send, the more fuel they use. In debt, you can't build.
  • The small chart under your money shows this week, Monday to Sunday. The solid line is what your customers paid so far, and the dotted line continues that pace to Sunday. The red dashed line is the bills you pay on Sunday. The number next to it is the result of the week at this pace: a profit if the dotted line ends above the red line, a loss if it ends below. When you build, the new bill shows for a moment. Tap the chart for the details.
  • Unlit homes slowly dim, and the bar under them shows the time left. If one goes fully dark, the game ends.
